Paper 01
Influence of Social Media Marketing Communications on Young Consumers' Attitudes
Rodney Graeme Duffett
2017 · Young Consumers Journal
2017 · Young Consumers Journal
#128279; doi.org/10.1108/YC-07-2016-00622
Research Problem
Gap in understanding how social media marketing communications (SMMC) influence cognitive, affective and behavioral attitudes among Generation Z in a developing-country context (South Africa).
Objectives
Examine SMMC influence on each attitude component; assess impact of usage variables (access, frequency, duration) and demographic variables (gender, age, ethnicity) on attitudes.
Methodology
Empirical quantitative survey using three self-administered questionnaires, one per attitude component. Generalized Linear Model (GLM) for analysis via SPSS.
Sample / Target
13,462 respondents Age 13–18 (Gen Z) Western Cape, SA Schools & Colleges
Paper 02
The Influence of Digital Marketing & Social Media Marketing on Consumer Buying Behavior
Barbara Antczak
2024 · Journal of Modern Science
2024 · Journal of Modern Science
#128279; jomswsge.com — Full PDF
Research Problem
How do digital marketing activities (SEO, email, social ads, influencer marketing) and AI-driven personalization shape consumer online purchasing decisions in the contemporary digital age?
Objectives
Explore relationship between digital/social media marketing and online purchasing behavior; examine role of AI tools; investigate how platform-specific features (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter) drive consumer action.
Methodology
Conceptual-analytical literature review synthesizing existing research, industry reports, and statistical data from Statista, BrightLocal, HubSpot, Sprout Social and others.
Sample / Target
Secondary data review Online consumers Poland / Global Age 7–75
Paper 03
Impact of Social Media on Consumer Buying Behavior
Sony Varghese & Mansi Agrawal
2021 · Saudi J Bus & Mgmt Studies
2021 · Saudi J Bus & Mgmt Studies
#128279; doi.org/10.36348/sjbms.2021.v06i03.001
Research Problem
How does social media influence complex consumer buying behavior (high-involvement, infrequent purchases) in urban India, and how does user-generated content alter the decision process?
Objectives
Study social media's impact on the six-stage EBM consumer decision process; measure perceptual change via content engagement; understand how brands can increase customer engagement and brand value.
Methodology
Quantitative primary research using structured questionnaire distributed to active online shoppers, selected via convenient sampling. Analysis using charts and descriptive statistics.
Sample / Target
200 respondents Urban India Online shoppers Multiple age groups

Key Findings
Paper 01 · Duffett
  • SMMC had a positive but declining effect on attitude components — mirroring the purchase funnel (awareness → purchase)
  • Teens who used social media longer and updated profiles more frequently showed the most favorable attitudinal responses
  • Colored and Black population groups showed strongest positive attitudes toward SMMC
  • 92.3% of Gen Z accessed social media via mobile devices
  • 62.4% logged on daily; attitudes were strongest among those with 5+ years of usage
  • Gender differences existed; females were more prevalent social media users (56.5%)
Paper 02 · Antczak
  • 54% of consumers use social media to research products before purchasing (Hootsuite, 2021)
  • 91% of consumers aged 18–34 trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations
  • 77% of consumers more likely to buy from brands they follow on social media
  • AI-driven personalization (Netflix, Zara, Coca-Cola) significantly boosts engagement and sales
  • Over 80% of consumers conduct online research before purchase; search engines and social media are top sources
  • Influencer marketing: 49% of consumers rely on influencer recommendations for purchasing decisions
Paper 03 · Varghese & Agrawal
  • Instagram (91.5%) and Facebook (91%) were the most used platforms among respondents
  • 64.5% said social media provides more information with easier access than traditional media
  • Consumers influenced by social media spend up to 4× more than in normal purchases
  • Discounts and promotions on social media strongly drive unplanned / impulse buying
  • Social media influencers create a positive impact on consumer buying decisions
  • Students are more engaged by ad frequency; salaried class responds more to visual display
Recommendations
Paper 01
Adapt SMMC strategies to declining purchase-funnel attitudinal stages
Target youth via mobile-first content; consider cultural demographics in South Africa
Brands targeting Gen Z must create engaging, creative content to cut through noise
Paper 02
Leverage AI for personalization and campaign optimization to boost ROI
Maintain strong social media presence to increase website traffic and conversions
Invest in trust-building: secure payment, reviews, transparent brand communication
Paper 03
Prioritize social media marketing to raise brand visibility and customer loyalty
Choose influencers who connect authentically with target consumer needs
Use promotions/discounts strategically to trigger unplanned purchases
Limitations
P1 Social media analyzed collectively, not by individual platform type; only Gen Z cohort studied
P1 Limited to Western Cape; findings may not generalize to all of South Africa or other regions
P2 Conceptual review lacks primary empirical data; dependent on secondary sources that may vary in reliability
P2 Poland-centric statistics may not apply globally; AI discussion largely theoretical
P3 Small sample (n=200); convenient sampling limits representativeness across India
P3 Focused only on urban consumers; rural and semi-urban behavior excluded from scope
